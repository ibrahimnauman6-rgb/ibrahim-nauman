## Why the size question comes before the price

A lot of the enquiries we get at Enpower start with one line: "What's the 50 kVA generator price in Pakistan?" It's a fair question. The trouble is that a good number of the people asking it don't need a 50 at all.

Some need a 30. Others need something bigger once their summer load is counted properly. A sharp quote for the wrong size is still a bad buy, so spend an afternoon checking whether 50 kVA is really where your building sits. Here's how we'd go about it.

Start with what the badge means. Your equipment draws real power, and at the usual 0.8 power factor a 50 kVA set gives about 40 kW. That's the number to hold your load list against, not the 50.

Then leave some room. We don't like to see a set sitting at full rating for hours in a Multan or Sukkur June, with hot air already going into the radiator. Headroom is what stops it tripping on the one afternoon you can't afford it.

## Measure in June, not in March

The most common sizing mistake we see is a load survey done in spring. The ACs are idle and the building looks light. Then the first 44°C week arrives with long hours of load-shedding, and the same building wants a lot more.

If you can, put a clamp meter on each phase of the main incomer on a hot afternoon, with everything on that would run during an outage. Planning in winter? List every AC, cooler and pump by its nameplate rating and add them back in.

Your electricity bill helps too. The sanctioned load on a LESCO, FESCO or K-Electric bill gives a rough ceiling. A maximum demand reading, if your bill shows one, is better still: it's what the building actually pulled at its busiest.

## Signs your site really is a 50 kVA job

In our experience, a 50 kVA set suits buildings where the load is a steady mix rather than one big machine: lighting and fans across a few floors, a handful of split ACs, computers, a water pump. The load moves through the day without huge jumps.

Two other things usually line up. The running load sits comfortably under 40 kW, with a margin for motor starts. And no single motor is large compared with the set, because a lift or a borewell pump can change the answer on its own.

## When 50 kVA is more than you need

Oversizing feels safe, but it has a cost. A set that spends its outages carrying a small fraction of its rating doesn't get hot enough to burn its fuel cleanly, and over the months that leads to wet stacking and sooty exhaust.

We see this most with offices that only back up lights, fans and IT, and shops that bought for an expansion that never happened. If your outage load is small and steady, a smaller set is usually the better buy.

## When 50 kVA won't be enough

The opposite problem shows up at start-up, not in the running figures. A direct-on-line motor can pull several times its running current as it starts. Put a lift, a submersible pump and a couple of older fixed-speed AC compressors on one set, and when two kick in together the voltage dips and contactors can drop out.

Inverter ACs are much gentler on a generator, which helps a lot of offices in Lahore and Karachi. Still, if your list includes a lift, a cold room or a heavy workshop motor, tell the dealer its kW rating and how it starts. That one detail can push you up a size, or show that a soft starter fixes it more cheaply.

## Does everything need to run on backup?

Here's a question that changes more quotes than any discount: what actually has to stay on? Plenty of buyers ask for "the whole building" and end up a size larger than they need.

Split the load into what must run during load-shedding and what can wait for WAPDA to come back. A clinic may need its lights, fridges, some ACs and the water pump, but not the electric water heaters. Wiring the essentials onto a separate board fed through the changeover can keep you inside the 50 kVA bracket, or let you drop below it.

## Getting a useful 50 kVA generator price in Pakistan

Once the size is settled, the 50 kVA generator price in Pakistan you're quoted starts to mean something. Send the dealer what you found, not just the number 50:

1. Your load list or clamp readings, with the date and time they were taken.
2. The largest motors on the list, their kW rating and how they start.
3. Expected running hours per day in the worst load-shedding months.
4. Where the set will sit, and whether neighbours mean you need a soundproof canopy.

With that, a dealer can tell you whether the Hyundai HDG50 or the Perkins EPG50 fits, or whether the honest answer is a size either side. We list both, and we'd rather talk a customer out of a 50 than sell the wrong set.
