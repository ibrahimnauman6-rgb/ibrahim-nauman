It usually happens at the worst moment. The grid drops a few minutes before Maghrib, the fans slow to a stop, the hall goes dark and the imam's microphone dies halfway through. Mosque committees in Lahore, Multan and Karachi have told us this story more times than we can count, and it's often what finally gets them to look at a proper backup set instead of a borrowed petrol unit and a long extension lead.

We at Enpower get asked about a Hyundai generator for mosques fairly often. Most neighbourhood mosques we've looked at fall in the smaller half of our ++[Hyundai diesel sets, each listed by kVA](https://enpower.com.pk/hyundai-diesel-generators/)++, but where exactly a mosque lands depends on things a shop counter quote never asks about. Here's how we'd think it through with a committee.

## A mosque's load follows the prayer times

A factory loads its set all shift. A mosque doesn't. Demand comes in five peaks a day, each lasting maybe half an hour to an hour, with long quiet gaps in between. Fajr is light: a few lights, some fans, the sound system. Zuhr and Asr in a Multan June are a different story, with every ceiling fan running and, in newer buildings, split ACs in the main hall as well.

Then there's Friday. Jumma brings the biggest crowd of the week, often spilling into the courtyard and the street, right at the hottest part of the afternoon. In Ramadan, Taraweeh adds a long evening run night after night for a month. Eid prayers are the single largest gathering of the year, even if they're early in the morning.

Write all of this down before anyone talks about kVA. A list of what's actually connected, room by room, beats any rule of thumb.

## Sizing a Hyundai generator for Jumma, not for Fajr

The set has to carry the heaviest realistic case, and for most mosques that's a summer Friday afternoon. Start with the steady loads: fans, lights, the sound system, water coolers, any ACs the committee wants on backup. Then look for motors, because they're what catch people out.

The obvious one is the water pump that fills the overhead tank for wudu. A pump motor pulls several times its running current for a moment when it starts. If it kicks in while the hall is full and every fan is on, a set that looked big enough on paper can dip hard enough to make the lights flicker and the sound system crackle. ACs do something similar every time their compressors cycle on.

Two fixes help. Put the pump on a timer so it fills the tank between prayers, not during them. And decide early whether the ACs belong on backup at all. Leaving them off the generator circuit can drop the size needed by a long way. That's a committee decision, not an engineering one, so ask it before you ask for quotes.

## Short runs, light loads and why the set needs exercise

This is the part most people miss. A diesel engine that keeps running for short spells at a small fraction of its rating doesn't burn its fuel cleanly. Over months, unburnt fuel and carbon build up in the exhaust and around the injectors. Engineers call it wet stacking. A Fajr run with a few lights and fans on a set sized for Jumma is close to the textbook case.

You can't avoid light loads completely, but you can stop them becoming the only thing the engine ever sees. Shift the tank filling onto generator time when the set is already running. And once a month, have it run for a while with a decent share of the building on it, not just idling in the courtyard. Your service provider can tell you what load and duration suit your particular set.

## Noise, smoke and the people outside

Most mosques sit in dense mohallas, with houses on three sides and a narrow lane on the fourth. A canopied Hyundai generator is a must here, not a luxury. Where it goes matters almost as much as how quiet it is.

Keep the exhaust pointing away from the wudu area, the main entrance and the windows of the prayer hall. Smoke drifting in during a summer Jumma, with every window open, is the complaint we'd most like committees to avoid. On a roof, check the slab can take the weight and that the exhaust clears the parapet. Leave room around the canopy for air, because a set boxed into a corner in 45°C heat will run hot.

## Keeping the sound system happy

The amplifier and microphones are usually the most sensitive thing in the building. They don't like voltage dips, and they're the first thing worshippers notice when something goes wrong.

A clean changeover helps. So does giving the set a short moment to settle before the heaviest loads come on, which an automatic transfer switch with a sensible delay can do. Good earthing on the generator and the mosque's own board matters as well, because a poorly earthed system is a common source of hum on the speakers. If the electrician who wired the mosque years ago isn't around, have someone check the earth before the set goes in.

## Who looks after it once the donors have paid

A mosque's Hyundai generator is usually paid for by donations, and that tends to shape how they're looked after. The purchase gets plenty of attention. The oil change eighteen months later, often, gets very little.

Pick one named person, usually the khadim or a committee member who lives nearby, and make the set their job. Keep a simple logbook next to it: run hours, fuel added, any alarms. Lock the fuel tank and the battery box. After an andhi, someone should blow the dust off the radiator before the next long run. And when the khadim changes, as they do, hand over the logbook and the keys properly, not just the generator.

A maintenance contract is worth considering here. Volunteers come and go. A service schedule that someone outside the mosque is responsible for keeps going when they do.

## Questions we'd ask before quoting

When a committee contacts us, these are the questions we work through first:

- Which rooms and circuits must stay on, and are the ACs among them?
- How big is the water pump, and can it run on a timer?
- Where can the set physically sit, and where will the exhaust go?
- Is there a madrassa, imam's quarters or shops on the same supply?
- Who will run it day to day, and who signs off on servicing?
